## Learn to Protect Yourself

Before learning how to hack things, learn how people actually get hacked.

A huge number of successful attacks start with something much simpler than a zero-day: stolen credentials, phishing, reused passwords or social engineering.

## Password managers

Stop reusing passwords.

[Bitwarden](https://bitwarden.com)

A free password manager can generate and store unique passwords for every account.

One strong master passphrase unlocks the vault. Everything else can be random and unique.

## Passkeys

Passkeys use cryptographic credentials stored on your device rather than a password that gets typed into a website.

They're designed to resist phishing because the credential is tied to the legitimate site or service.

Where a reputable service offers a passkey, it's generally worth considering.

## MFA

Turn it on everywhere it's offered:

* Email
* Gaming accounts
* Social media
* Banking
* Work accounts
* Cloud services

Prefer passkeys, hardware security keys or authenticator apps over SMS where practical.

Save your recovery codes somewhere safe.

## Learn to Spot the Human Side of Security

Security isn't just computers.

A huge part of the job is understanding how people are manipulated.

### The general pattern

A lot of scams contain the same ingredients:

!!! warning "The general pattern"
    Urgency + emotion + a request to do something unusual.

Slow down.

Thirty seconds of thinking can defeat a surprisingly large number of scams.

### Common examples

**Discord / gaming scams**

Fake Nitro, giveaways, game currency or support messages can lead to fake login pages designed to steal credentials.

**Account takeover**

A compromised friend's account asks you to "send me the code you just received."

That code may actually be the login code for **your** account.

Never give someone an authentication code because they asked for it.

**Fake cheats and mods**

"Aimbot", "mod menu", "free skins" and similar downloads are a common way of getting malware onto gaming PCs.

If software tells you to disable your antivirus before it will work, that's a massive red flag.

**Sextortion**

Someone builds trust, obtains intimate images or video, then threatens to distribute them unless you pay or send more.

If this happens to you or someone you know, see [If Something Actually Goes Wrong](if-something-goes-wrong.md) for what to do.

**AI voice cloning**

A convincing voice isn't proof that someone is who they claim to be.

For genuinely important requests, independently contact the person using a known number or another channel.
