# Find Your People

Most people don't get their first break in cyber security from a job board. They get it from someone they already knew — a meetup, a Discord, a person who saw their CTF write-up and mentioned an opening.

Reading and labbing on your own gets you the skills. Showing up somewhere regularly is what turns those skills into opportunities.

!!! tip "Pick one, not all of these"
    You don't need to be active everywhere. Showing up consistently to one community beats lurking in ten.

## In person (Victoria / Australia)

* [BSides Melbourne](https://www.bsidesmelbourne.com) — a community-run, volunteer-organised security conference, deliberately built to be welcoming to first-time speakers and students, not just seasoned professionals
* [AWSN — Australian Women in Security Network](https://www.awsn.org.au) — events, mentoring and scholarships aimed at women and girls in security, running nationally including a Victorian chapter
* [OWASP Melbourne](https://owasp.org/www-chapter-melbourne/) — application security-focused meetup, useful if the "building things securely" side of the field (rather than breaking them) is more your speed

If you're outside Victoria, search "[your city] cyber security meetup" or "[your city] OWASP chapter" — most capital cities have something running.

## Online

* [r/cybersecurity](https://www.reddit.com/r/cybersecurity/) — general discussion, career questions, "is this a scam" sanity checks
* [TryHackMe's official Discord](https://discord.com/invite/tryhackme) — active while you're working through rooms, useful for getting unstuck without just looking up the answer
* LinkedIn — not just for job hunting. Post about what you've built ([Build Evidence, Not Certificates](build-evidence.md) gives you the material), and follow people actually working in the area of security you're interested in

## What to actually do with these

Ask questions. Answer questions once you can. Post the CTF write-up. Comment on someone else's.

Nobody remembers the person who lurked for a year. People remember the person who was consistently, visibly curious.
