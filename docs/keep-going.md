# Keep Going

Cyber security is one of the few fields where you can start learning almost immediately.

You can:

* Download free software
* Build a lab
* Play CTFs
* Write code
* Investigate malware
* Learn networking
* Analyse logs
* Read threat intelligence
* Break deliberately vulnerable machines
* Build things
* Share what you've learned

You don't need to wait until university.

You don't need an expensive certification.

You don't need a job in cyber.

You need curiosity.

The people who end up getting good at this are usually the ones who kept asking:

!!! quote ""
    "I wonder what happens if I do this?"

Just make sure you're doing it somewhere you're actually allowed to.

## One honest thing before you go

Nobody talks about this enough, so it's going here.

Cyber security burns people out. Alert fatigue from tools that cry wolf all day. The slow-building sense that there's always more bad guys than there is budget or headcount to deal with them. The feeling that if you look away for a week, something will slip through.

That feeling is real, and it happens to people who are good at this job, not just people who aren't cut out for it.

It doesn't go away by working harder. It gets managed by:

* Actually taking the leave you're owed
* Talking to someone — a colleague, a mentor, a professional — rather than sitting with it alone
* Accepting that you will not catch everything, and that's true of every security team everywhere, not a personal failure
* Remembering that this page exists because you got curious about something, not because you signed up to carry the weight of the entire internet

If you're already in the industry and this is hitting close to home: that's worth a conversation with someone you trust, sooner rather than later. Burnout is an occupational hazard here, not a personal weakness.
