# If You Want to Take It Further

A university degree isn't required to start learning cyber security.

There are several routes.

### University

* La Trobe University (Bendigo) — Cyber Security / IT
* Deakin University — Cyber Security
* RMIT University — Cybersecurity
* Federation University — Cyber security programs
* Other universities across Australia offer cyber security, computer science and related degrees

### TAFE / VET

TAFE and other vocational providers offer shorter, more practical pathways into IT and cyber security. Locally, Bendigo TAFE and GOTAFE both run IT and cyber-related courses, and are worth a look if you want something more hands-on and less lecture-hall than a full degree.

### Victorian Government Cyber Internship Program

[Build your cyber career with the Victorian Government](https://www.vic.gov.au/build-your-cyber-career-victorian-government)

Worth calling out on its own: this is a real, walked pathway, not just a link on a list. A previous work experience participant went on to complete a Certificate IV in Cyber Security and is now working in the Cyber Incident Response Service at the Department of Government Services.

The program is part of the Free TAFE initiative — it takes recent Certificate IV in Cyber Security graduates and places them working alongside Victorian Government cyber teams in one of several streams: incident response, cyber awareness and education, threat intelligence, GRC, or security operations. Expressions of interest open periodically (they're not year-round), so check the page above for current dates rather than assuming it's always open.

If you're weighing up whether TAFE is "worth it" compared to jumping straight into a job, this is a concrete answer: TAFE plus this internship is a genuine, funded route from "no experience" to "working in cyber for the Victorian Government" in a relatively short timeframe.

### If you want a certification anyway

[Build Evidence, Not Certificates](build-evidence.md) is the philosophy on this site, and it still stands — a portfolio beats a wall of certificates. But a couple of entry-level certs genuinely help get an application noticed, especially with zero professional experience to point to yet:

* **CompTIA A+** — not a security cert at all, it's general IT. But it's a solid "jack of all trades, master of none" starting point that signals you understand how a computer actually works, which is exactly the foundation [Get the Foundations](foundations.md) is built on.
* **CompTIA Security+** — the standard entry-level security cert, widely recognised and often literally listed as a minimum requirement in junior security job ads.
* **ISC2 Certified in Cybersecurity (CC)** — a genuinely free entry-level cert from a major industry body, worth doing purely because of the price.
* **Microsoft SC-900** — useful specifically if you're heading toward a Microsoft-shop environment (which is most Australian workplaces), covers security, compliance and identity concepts at a foundational level.

Pick one, not all four. Then go build the evidence to back it up.
